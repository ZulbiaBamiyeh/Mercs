Option A

Card table

The current layout, rebuilt around tall cards. Enemies on top and your mercs below, with a turn-order strip between them. The skill tray becomes a bottom sheet that shows every skill's text, so nothing has to be tapped just to read it.

  • Cards fill the width: about 112 by 155 px, against 96 by 120 for today's ovals.
  • The order strip replaces the speech bubbles and reads left to right.
  • Every skill's text is visible at a glance.

Option B

Hero focus

One merc at a time, big. The enemy row shrinks to a strip. Your active merc fills the middle like a Reigns card, and its three skills sit underneath as small cards. Tabs switch between your mercs.

  • The art gets the space it deserves.
  • Skills read like cards, with room for longer text.
  • You don't see all six stat lines at once, so it needs the order strip or an overview button.

Option C

Lanes

Each of your mercs faces an enemy across the table. Skill buttons sit right under each card, so you give orders without opening a tray, and the chosen skill's text runs along the bottom.

  • Fewest taps: every order is one tap away.
  • Reads as a duel, which suits the speed-race idea.
  • The lanes are visual only; any merc can still target any enemy. Only the chosen skill's text is shown.

Polish ideas, whichever layout wins

  1. Rectangular cards. Tall cards (roughly 3:4) fit a phone's width far better than ovals, and suit the flat art, which is already composed as rectangles.
  2. Bigger numbers, fewer things. Stats at 26-30 px, names under each card, one status row. Hide the log behind a button on phones.
  3. A turn-order strip. A face-chip timeline instead of speech bubbles: clearer on a small screen, and it animates nicely as each unit acts.
  4. Skill text always visible. On phones, show skills as rows or cards with their text, so tap-to-read goes away.
  5. One frame language. Swap the gold-oval-plus-gems frame for a thin brass card border and flat stat badges, closer to the flat art. Keep the gloss for buttons only.
  6. Juice. Cards tilt toward their target when attacking, a hit-stop of a few frames on crits, damage numbers that pop and settle, and a short screen flash on kills.
  7. Haptics and sound. A small vibration on hits and on Ready, where the device allows it, plus softer, woodier UI sounds to match the tabletop.
  8. Onboarding. A 30-second first fight with three fixed mercs and hints, before the party screen.
